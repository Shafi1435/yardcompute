How many bags of concrete do I need?

Bagged concrete mix is sold by weight, but what matters for your project is volume. Each bag makes a set amount of mixed concrete, called its yield. This calculator works out the volume of a rectangular slab or pad, adds a waste allowance, and divides by the yield of one bag to give a whole number of bags.

How to use the concrete bag calculator

  1. Enter the length and width of the area in feet.
  2. Enter the thickness in inches. Many walkways and small patios are 4 in thick.
  3. Enter the yield printed on your bag in cubic feet. Check the bag, because yield varies by product and bag size.
  4. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Base volume (cu ft) = length × width × (thickness ÷ 12)
Order volume = base volume × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)
Cubic yards = order volume ÷ 27
Bags = round up (order volume ÷ bag yield)

Example

The default values describe a 10 ft × 10 ft pad, 4 in thick, using bags that yield 0.6 cu ft. The base volume is 10 × 10 × 4/12 = 33.33 cu ft. With 10% extra, that is 36.67 cu ft, or 1.36 cubic yards. 36.67 ÷ 0.6 = 61.1, so the calculator suggests 62 bags.

If you switch to smaller bags with a 0.45 cu ft yield, the same pad needs 36.67 ÷ 0.45 = 81.5, or 82 bags.

Typical bag yields

Many standard concrete mixes list a yield of about 0.60 cu ft for an 80 lb bag, about 0.45 cu ft for a 60 lb bag, and about 0.30 cu ft for a 40 lb bag. These are typical figures, not guarantees. High-strength, fast-setting, and specialty mixes can differ, so always use the yield printed on the bag you are buying.

Bags or ready-mix?

Bags are practical for small jobs, but the count climbs quickly. Once a project reaches roughly a cubic yard or more, many people compare the cost and labor of mixing bags with a ready-mix delivery. Ask local suppliers about minimum orders and short-load fees before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Why add a waste allowance?

Uneven ground, a slightly deeper base, spills, and form movement all use extra concrete. Running short in the middle of a pour can leave a weak cold joint, so a small allowance is cheap insurance.

Does this include gravel base?

No. This counts only the concrete. A compacted gravel base under the slab is a separate material; use the Gravel Calculator for it.

Can I use this for round post holes?

Not directly, because this calculator assumes a rectangle. Use the Post Hole Concrete Calculator or the Concrete Footing Calculator for round holes.

How do I handle an L-shaped slab?

Split it into rectangles, calculate each one, and add the bag counts together.

Can I enter metric sizes?

Yes. Change the length and width to meters and the thickness to centimeters, and the calculator converts them. Bag yield stays in cubic feet, as printed on most US bags.
