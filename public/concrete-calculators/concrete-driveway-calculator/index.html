Concrete Driveway Calculator

Estimate concrete volume and cubic yards for a rectangular driveway using slab dimensions and a waste allowance.

How this calculator works

Enter measured project dimensions and planning inputs. The calculator converts them into the quantities shown in the results.

Formula

Concrete volume = length × width × slab thickness in feet. Order volume applies the selected waste allowance, then cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27.

Example

For a 40 ft × 12 ft driveway at 5 in thick, the base volume is 200 cu ft before waste.

Assumptions and material notes

This is a rectangular slab planning estimate. It does not design reinforcement, subbase, joints, slopes, or thickened edges.

Worked example in full

The default values describe a 40 ft × 12 ft driveway, 5 in thick. The area is 480 sq ft and the base volume is 480 × 5/12 = 200 cu ft. With a 10% allowance, the order volume is 220 cu ft, or about 8.15 cubic yards.

A two-car driveway 40 ft × 20 ft at the same thickness has 800 sq ft and needs about 13.58 cubic yards with 10% extra. At 6 in thick, the single-car example rises to 240 cu ft before waste, or about 9.78 cubic yards with 10% extra.

Driveway thickness and base

Driveways carry vehicles, so they are usually thicker than patios and walkways, and they rest on a compacted gravel subbase. The right thickness depends on vehicle weight, soil, climate, and local rules. Heavier vehicles such as RVs or trucks often call for a thicker slab or reinforcement. Use the thickness from your plan or contractor, and estimate the subbase with the Driveway Gravel Calculator.

Ordering ready-mix for a driveway

  • Most driveways are far too large for bagged concrete. Ready-mix is usually ordered by the cubic yard.
  • Trucks have a maximum load, so large driveways may need more than one truck. Ask your supplier how they schedule multiple loads.
  • Round your order up to the supplier's increment. Running short mid-pour is harder to fix than a small leftover.
  • Measure the actual depth of the forms in several places. A slightly deeper subgrade adds up quickly over a large area.

Frequently asked questions

What thickness should I enter?

Enter the planned slab thickness from your project design or specifications.

Does it include waste?

Yes. The waste field is applied to the base concrete volume.

How are cubic yards calculated?

Cubic feet are divided by 27.

Does it calculate reinforcement?

No. Reinforcement and structural details are outside this volume estimate.

Should I verify the order?

Yes. Recheck measurements and confirm the final quantity with your supplier.

How many cubic yards is a typical single-car driveway?

It depends on size and thickness. The default 40 ft × 12 ft driveway at 5 in thick needs about 8.15 cubic yards with 10% extra.

Does a sloped driveway need more concrete?

The volume depends on the slab area and thickness, not the slope. Measure the length along the surface of the slope for the best estimate.
