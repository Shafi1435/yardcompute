How much concrete do I need for round footings?

Round footings are common under pergola posts, porch piers, sheds, signs, and other structures. Each one is a short cylinder dug into the ground. This calculator finds the volume of one footing from its diameter and depth, multiplies by the number of footings, adds waste, and estimates how many 80 lb bags that would take.

How to use the concrete footing calculator

  1. Enter the number of footings that share the same size.
  2. Enter the hole or form diameter in inches.
  3. Enter the depth of concrete in inches. Measure the actual hole depth, not just the plan depth.
  4. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Volume of one footing = π × (diameter ÷ 24)² × (depth ÷ 12)
Base concrete = one footing × number of footings
Order volume = base × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)
Approx. 80 lb bags = round up (order volume ÷ 0.60)

Example

The default values describe 4 footings, each 18 in across and 24 in deep. One footing holds π × 0.75² × 2 = 3.53 cu ft, so four hold 14.14 cu ft. With 10% extra, the order volume is 15.55 cu ft, or 0.58 cubic yards. At about 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag, that is 26 bags.

Depth and frost line

In cold climates, footings usually need to reach below the local frost depth so the ground cannot heave them. Required depth and diameter vary widely by location, soil, and load, so check with your local building department before digging. The deeper the hole, the more concrete each footing needs, and the effect adds up quickly over several footings.

Things that change the estimate

  • Belled or flared bottoms. A wider base holds more concrete than a straight cylinder.
  • Irregular holes. Auger holes in rocky or sandy soil are often wider than the auger. Measure a few holes and use the average.
  • Gravel at the bottom. If you put gravel in the bottom of the hole, subtract that depth.
  • Different bag sizes. The bag estimate assumes 80 lb bags at about 0.60 cu ft. Use the cubic-foot result with your own bag yield if it differs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between this and the Deck Footing Calculator?

The math is the same. The Deck Footing Calculator is set up with typical deck pier defaults and deck-specific notes. Use whichever matches your project.

What about fence posts?

For fence posts, where the post itself takes up space in the hole, use the Fence Post Concrete Calculator or Post Hole Concrete Calculator.

Should I order ready-mix for footings?

For a few footings, bags are usually simpler. For many large footings, compare the bag count with a ready-mix delivery and ask about minimum orders.

Can I enter metric sizes?

Yes. Switch diameter and depth to centimeters, and the calculator converts them.

Is this enough for a permit?

No. It is a quantity estimate. Footing size and depth for permitted work must follow local code and approved plans.
