How much concrete does a round column need?

Round concrete columns, such as porch columns or piers formed with cardboard tube forms, are cylinders. The volume of each column is the area of its circular cross-section times its height. This calculator works that out for one column, multiplies by the number of columns, and adds a waste allowance.

How to use the concrete column calculator

  1. Enter how many columns are the same size. Calculate different sizes separately.
  2. Enter the inside diameter of the form in inches.
  3. Enter the column height in feet, including any part that sits below grade.
  4. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Radius (ft) = diameter in inches ÷ 24
Volume of one column = π × radius² × height
Base concrete = one column × number of columns
Order volume = base × (1 + waste % ÷ 100); cubic yards = order volume ÷ 27

Example

The default values describe 4 columns, each 12 in in diameter and 8 ft tall. The radius is 0.5 ft, so one column holds π × 0.5² × 8 = 6.28 cu ft. Four columns hold 25.13 cu ft. With 10% extra, the order volume is 27.65 cu ft, or 1.02 cubic yards.

Diameter has a big effect. Going from a 12 in to a 16 in form makes the cross-section about 1.8 times larger, so the concrete needed grows by about 78% for the same height.

Columns vs. footings

This calculator is for tall, above-grade cylinders. For short cylinders that sit in the ground under a post or pier, the Concrete Footing Calculator takes depth in inches and also estimates bags. If a column sits on a wider spread footing, calculate the footing separately and add the two volumes.

Things to plan for

  • Reinforcing steel and anchor hardware take up a small amount of volume but are usually ignored in the estimate.
  • Tall columns are often poured in one continuous pour. Make sure you have enough mixed concrete ready before you start.
  • Tube forms need bracing so they stay plumb while the concrete is placed.
  • Structural columns must be designed to carry their load. Check plans and local code with a qualified professional.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use the inside or outside diameter of the form?

Use the inside diameter, because that is the shape of the concrete.

Can I order this as bagged concrete?

Yes. Divide the order volume in cubic feet by your bag yield, or use the Concrete Bag Calculator logic: about 0.6 cu ft per 80 lb bag is a common figure, but check your bag.

How do I handle square columns?

Use length × width × height instead of the circle formula. The Concrete Calculator handles rectangular shapes.

Can I enter metric sizes?

Yes. Switch the diameter to centimeters and the height to meters, and the calculator converts them.

Does this include waste for spills?

Yes, through the waste allowance input. 5–10% is a common planning range.
