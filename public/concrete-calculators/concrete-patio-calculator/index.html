How much concrete do I need for a patio?

A patio slab is a flat rectangle, so its volume is simply length × width × thickness. This calculator finds the patio area and concrete volume, adds a waste allowance, and converts the result to cubic yards, the unit ready-mix suppliers use.

How to use the concrete patio calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the patio inside the forms.
  2. Enter the slab thickness in inches. 4 in is common for patios that carry foot traffic and furniture.
  3. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Patio area = length × width
Base concrete (cu ft) = area × (thickness ÷ 12)
Order volume = base × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)
Cubic yards = order volume ÷ 27

Example

The default values describe a 20 ft × 12 ft patio, 4 in thick. The area is 240 sq ft, and the base volume is 240 × 4/12 = 80 cu ft. With 10% extra, the order volume is 88 cu ft, or 3.26 cubic yards.

Thickness matters more than it looks. The same patio at 5 in thick needs 100 cu ft before waste, or 4.07 cubic yards with 10% extra. That extra inch adds a quarter more concrete.

Planning a patio pour

  • Base. Most patios sit on a compacted gravel base. Estimate it separately with the Gravel Calculator.
  • Slope. Patios are usually sloped slightly away from the house for drainage, which can make one edge a little thicker.
  • Thickened edges. If the slab has a thickened edge or footing around the perimeter, add that volume.
  • Ordering. Suppliers often sell in quarter- or half-yard steps and may charge a fee for small loads. Ask before you order.
  • Bags. At 3 or more cubic yards, bagged concrete means well over 100 bags. Ready-mix is often more practical at this size.

Frequently asked questions

Is 4 in thick enough for a patio?

For many foot-traffic patios, yes. Hot tubs, outdoor kitchens, or poor soil may call for a thicker slab or reinforcement. Check local guidance or a contractor for your situation.

How do I handle a curved or irregular patio?

Break it into rectangles and simple shapes, calculate each one, and add the volumes. For curves, a slightly larger rectangle gives a safe estimate.

How much waste should I add?

A 5–10% allowance is a common range. Use more if the subgrade is uneven or the forms are not perfectly level.

Can I enter metric sizes?

Yes. Switch length and width to meters and thickness to centimeters.

How many bags would this be?

Divide the order volume in cubic feet by your bag yield, or use the Concrete Bag Calculator.
