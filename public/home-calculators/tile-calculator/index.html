How many tiles do I need?

This calculator finds the area of a rectangular floor or wall, divides it by the area of one tile, and adds a waste allowance for cuts and breakage. It gives a whole number of tiles that you can turn into boxes.

How to use the tile calculator

  1. Enter the length and width of the area to tile, in feet.
  2. Enter the tile length and width in inches.
  3. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Project area = length × width
Tile area (sq ft) = (tile length ÷ 12) × (tile width ÷ 12)
Tiles = round up (project area ÷ tile area × (1 + waste % ÷ 100))

Example

The default values describe a 12 ft × 10 ft floor and 12 × 12 in tiles. The floor is 120 sq ft and each tile is 1 sq ft, so the floor takes 120 tiles. With 10% extra, plan for 132 tiles.

With 12 × 24 in tiles, each covers 2 sq ft, so the same floor needs 66 tiles with 10% extra.

Choosing a waste allowance

  • Straight layouts with standard tiles: about 10% is a common planning figure.
  • Diagonal layouts, herringbone, and large-format tiles: often 15% or more.
  • Rooms with many corners, pipes, or a toilet flange: add a little more.
  • Keep a few spare tiles after the job for future repairs.

Grout joints

The calculator uses the tile size and ignores grout joints. Joints slightly reduce the number of tiles needed, so ignoring them adds a small safety margin. For very small tiles with wide joints, the difference is bigger.

Turning tiles into boxes

Tile is usually sold by the box, with coverage in square feet on the label. Divide the area with waste by the box coverage and round up. Buy all boxes from the same lot, because color and size can vary between production runs.

Frequently asked questions

Does this work for wall tile?

Yes. Enter the wall width and height as the length and width. Subtract windows or large openings from the area.

How do I handle an L-shaped room?

Split it into rectangles, calculate each one, and add the tile counts.

Does this include thinset and grout?

No. Those are separate materials. Their coverage depends on tile size, trowel notch, and joint width; check the product labels.

Can I use metric?

Yes. Switch the area to meters and the tile size to centimeters.

What about plank-style flooring?

Use the Flooring Calculator with the box coverage.
