How much paint do I need for a room?

Paint coverage is listed per gallon, so the job is to work out how many square feet you are painting. This calculator finds the wall area from the room size, subtracts doors and windows, multiplies by the number of coats, divides by the coverage per gallon, and rounds up to whole gallons.

How to use the paint calculator

  1. Enter the room length, width, and wall height in feet.
  2. Enter the combined area of doors and windows in square feet.
  3. Enter the number of coats. Two is common, especially when changing color.
  4. Enter the coverage per gallon from the paint can. 350 sq ft per gallon is a typical label figure, but it varies.
  5. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Gross wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height
Net paint area = gross area − openings
Gallons = round up (net area × coats ÷ coverage × (1 + waste % ÷ 100))

Example

The default values describe a 12 ft × 10 ft room with 8 ft walls and 40 sq ft of openings. The walls total 352 sq ft, or 312 sq ft after openings. Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon need 312 × 2 ÷ 350 = 1.78 gallons, or about 1.96 with 10% extra, so plan for 2 gallons.

A larger 16 ft × 14 ft room with the same openings has 440 sq ft to paint, which needs 2.77 gallons with two coats and waste, so 3 gallons.

When you need more paint

  • Bare drywall, patched walls, or porous surfaces absorb more paint. A primer coat helps.
  • Big color changes, especially light over dark, may need a third coat.
  • Textured walls and rough surfaces have more surface area than flat walls.
  • Rollers with thick nap hold more paint.

What this does not include

Ceilings, trim, doors, and cabinets are not counted. For a ceiling, add length × width and calculate it separately with ceiling paint's coverage. Trim paint is usually bought in quarts.

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy gallons or a 5-gallon bucket?

For large jobs, a 5-gallon bucket can cost less per gallon. It also keeps the color consistent across the whole job.

Do I need primer?

Often for new drywall, stains, or big color changes. Primer coverage is listed on its own can; calculate it as one coat.

Why is coverage only an estimate?

The figure on the can assumes a smooth, sealed surface. Real coverage depends on the surface, color, and how the paint is applied.

Can I use metric?

Yes. Switch the room dimensions to meters. Openings and coverage stay in square feet.
