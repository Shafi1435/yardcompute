How do I lay out stair stringers?

A stair stringer is the notched board that carries the treads. To lay one out, you need the number of steps, the exact height of each riser, the total horizontal run, and the length of board. This calculator divides the total rise into equal risers no taller than your target, then works out the run and the approximate stringer length.

How to use the stair stringer calculator

  1. Measure the total rise in inches: from the finished lower surface to the finished upper surface.
  2. Enter your target maximum riser height.
  3. Enter the tread depth, the horizontal distance from one riser to the next.
  4. Select Calculate.

Formula

Risers = round up (total rise ÷ target riser height)
Actual riser height = total rise ÷ risers
Treads = risers − 1
Total run = treads × tread depth
Stringer length ≈ √(total rise² + total run²)

Example

The default values use a total rise of 96 in, a 7.5 in target riser, and 10 in treads. 96 ÷ 7.5 = 12.8, which rounds up to 13 risers, each 96 ÷ 13 = 7.38 in tall. Thirteen risers have 12 treads, so the run is 12 × 10 = 120 in. The stringer runs along the diagonal: √(96² + 120²) ≈ 153.7 in, or about 12.81 ft.

Why treads are one less than risers

The top step is the upper floor or deck itself, so it is not cut into the stringer. That is why a stair with 13 risers has 12 treads. Some designs differ, for example when the stringer hangs below the upper deck, so check your detail.

Code and safety

Stair rules matter. Many US residential codes based on the International Residential Code limit riser height to about 7¾ in and require treads of at least 10 in, with very little variation allowed between steps. Rules vary by location, and handrails, guards, headroom, and landings have their own requirements. Check with your local building department before you build.

Buying the stringer board

The stringer length is the diagonal only. Buy a board a bit longer to allow for the top and bottom cuts, and choose a lumber width that leaves enough solid wood below the notches. Most stairs use at least two stringers, with more for wide stairs.

Frequently asked questions

Should I measure rise to the finished floor?

Yes. Measure from finished surface to finished surface. If flooring will be added later, include its thickness or the first or last step will be uneven.

Can I change the riser height?

Yes. Lower the target riser height to get more, shorter steps. The calculator will add risers and lengthen the run.

What about the nosing?

Tread nosing overhangs the riser below and does not change the stringer layout. The tread board itself is usually deeper than the run.

Can I use metric?

Yes. Switch the inputs to centimeters; results display in inches and feet.

Does this design a landing?

No. For stairs with a landing, calculate each flight separately.
