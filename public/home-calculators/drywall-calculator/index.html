How many drywall sheets do I need?

This calculator estimates drywall for the walls of a rectangular room. It finds the total wall area from the room perimeter and wall height, subtracts doors and windows, adds a waste allowance, and divides by the area of one sheet.

How to use the drywall calculator

  1. Enter the room length, width, and wall height in feet.
  2. Enter the combined area of doors and windows in square feet. A standard interior door is roughly 20 sq ft.
  3. Enter the sheet area. A 4 × 8 ft sheet is 32 sq ft and a 4 × 12 ft sheet is 48 sq ft.
  4. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Gross wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height
Net wall area = gross wall area − openings
Area with waste = net area × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)
Sheets = round up (area with waste ÷ sheet area)

Example

The default values describe a 16 ft × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls and 40 sq ft of doors and windows. The walls total 2 × (16 + 12) × 8 = 448 sq ft, or 408 sq ft after openings. With 10% extra, that is 448.8 sq ft. Divided by 32 sq ft sheets, the calculator suggests 15 sheets.

The same room with 4 × 12 ft sheets needs 448.8 ÷ 48 = 9.35, or 10 sheets. Larger sheets mean fewer seams to tape, but they are heavier and harder to carry through doors.

What this does not include

  • Ceilings. Add length × width for the ceiling and calculate it separately. Ceilings often use a different, lighter sheet.
  • Joint compound, tape, screws, and corner bead. These are separate purchases.
  • Different board types. Bathrooms, garages, and some walls may need moisture-resistant or fire-rated board. Calculate those walls separately.

Choosing a waste allowance

Offcuts around windows, doors, and outlets are hard to reuse. A 10–15% allowance is a common planning range for rooms with several openings. Simple rooms with few openings can use less, while rooms with many angles, soffits, or short walls may need more.

Frequently asked questions

Should I subtract all openings?

Subtract large openings like doors and big windows. Many people do not subtract small windows, because the cut-out piece is rarely reusable.

What size sheet should I use?

Choose sheets that suit your wall height and how you will carry them in. 4 × 8 ft is easiest to handle; longer sheets reduce seams.

Does wall height above 8 ft change anything?

Yes. Taller walls increase the area and may need sheets hung differently. Enter the real height and the area updates.

Can I use metric?

Yes. Switch the room dimensions to meters. Openings and sheet area stay in square feet.
