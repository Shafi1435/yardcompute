How to Calculate Post Hole Concrete

Post hole concrete can be estimated from the number of holes, the hole diameter, and the hole depth. The same basic cylinder-volume method works for fence posts, signs, mailbox posts, and other cylindrical post holes.

1. Measure the post holes

Record the number of holes and the planned diameter and depth of each hole. If holes have different sizes, calculate each group separately and add the results.

2. Use the cylinder formula

For one cylindrical hole:

Volume = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth

Keep all dimensions in the same unit before calculating. For cubic feet, convert inches to feet first. For example, a 12-inch diameter by 24-inch deep hole has a volume of about 1.57 cubic feet.

3. Multiply by the number of holes

Multiply the volume of one hole by the number of holes. Ten holes at 1.57 cubic feet each require about 15.71 cubic feet before any allowance.

4. Add a practical allowance

Site conditions, irregular excavation, soil loss, and installation differences can increase actual concrete use. A 10% planning allowance is a common estimate, but the appropriate amount depends on the project.

5. Convert volume to concrete bags

If buying bagged concrete, divide the required cubic-foot volume by the yield per bag. Bag yields vary by product and bag size, so use the yield printed on the product label rather than assuming every 60- or 80-pound bag has the same yield.

Quick example

Suppose you have 4 holes, each 12 inches in diameter and 24 inches deep.

One hole: π × (1 ft ÷ 2)² × 2 ft ≈ 1.57 cu ft.

Four holes: 1.57 × 4 ≈ 6.28 cu ft.

With a 10% allowance: 6.28 × 1.10 ≈ 6.91 cu ft.

You can then compare 6.91 cubic feet with the stated yield of your chosen concrete bag.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing inches, feet, centimeters, or meters in one calculation.
  • Forgetting to multiply the single-hole volume by the number of holes.
  • Using a generic bag yield instead of the manufacturer's stated yield.
  • Ignoring extra volume caused by irregular or oversized holes.
  • Rounding intermediate values too early.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate concrete for a round post hole?

Use the cylinder formula with the hole diameter and depth, then multiply by the number of holes.

Should I include a waste allowance?

For planning, an allowance can help account for irregular holes and site conditions. The right amount depends on the project.

Can I use this for metric measurements?

Yes. Keep diameter and depth in the same metric unit and convert the final volume to the unit needed for ordering.

How many bags do I need?

Divide the total required volume by the concrete yield per bag. Always use the yield printed by the manufacturer.

What if my post holes are different sizes?

Calculate each hole size or group separately, then add the volumes before converting to bags.
