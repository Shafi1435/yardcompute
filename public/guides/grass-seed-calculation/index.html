How to Calculate Grass Seed for a Lawn

Grass seed quantity is usually based on lawn area and the seeding rate recommended for the specific seed product. Because rates differ by grass type, application purpose, and product, the package label should be the starting point.

1. Calculate lawn area

For a rectangular lawn, multiply length by width. If the lawn has several simple sections, calculate each section and add them together. Irregular areas can be approximated by dividing them into smaller shapes.

2. Find the product's seeding rate

Look for the recommended rate on the seed label. It may be expressed as pounds per 1,000 square feet or another unit. Keep the area and rate in compatible units.

3. Apply the formula

If the rate is given in pounds per 1,000 square feet:

Seed pounds = lawn area ÷ 1,000 × seeding rate

For example, a 5,000 square-foot lawn at 6 lb per 1,000 square feet requires:

5,000 ÷ 1,000 × 6 = 30 lb

New lawn versus overseeding

Do not assume the same rate applies to every situation. A product may specify different rates for establishing a new lawn and overseeding an existing lawn. Use the rate that matches your application.

Account for actual lawn shape

Driveways, patios, planting beds, buildings, and other areas that will not be seeded should be excluded from the lawn area. Measuring these separately can make the estimate more useful than treating the entire property as lawn.

Common mistakes

  • Using a generic seed rate instead of the product label.
  • Forgetting to subtract hardscape and planting areas.
  • Mixing square feet with a rate stated for another unit.
  • Using a new-lawn rate for overseeding or vice versa.
  • Buying by bag count without checking the pounds per bag.

FAQ

How much seed do I need for 1,000 square feet?

It depends on the product's recommended rate. At 6 lb per 1,000 square feet, the estimate would be 6 lb.

Should I use the same rate for overseeding?

Not necessarily. Follow the product label for the intended application.

Can I calculate bags instead of pounds?

Yes. First calculate pounds required, then divide by the pounds of seed in the package and round up to whole bags when purchasing.
