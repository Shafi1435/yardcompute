How to Calculate Gravel for a Driveway

Driveway gravel estimates depend on the driveway area, the planned gravel depth, and the material's bulk density. Start by calculating volume, then use the gravel density to estimate weight.

1. Measure the driveway

Measure the driveway length and average width. If the driveway is irregular, divide it into simple sections and calculate each section separately.

2. Choose the gravel depth

Measure or specify the depth for the layer you are estimating. Keep the depth in a compatible unit with the length and width before calculating volume. If a driveway uses multiple gravel layers, calculate each layer separately rather than treating all layers as one material.

3. Calculate cubic feet

For a rectangular section, use:

Volume = length × width × depth

If length and width are in feet and depth is in inches, convert inches to feet by dividing by 12.

Example: a 40 ft × 12 ft driveway with 4 inches of gravel has a depth of 4 ÷ 12 = 0.3333 ft.

40 × 12 × 0.3333 = about 160 cubic feet

4. Add a planning allowance

A material allowance can help account for measurement variation, compaction, spreading, and waste. For a 10% allowance, multiply the calculated volume by 1.10.

160 × 1.10 = 176 cubic feet

5. Convert gravel volume to tons

Gravel suppliers may sell material by weight. To estimate tons, multiply the cubic-foot volume by the gravel's bulk density in pounds per cubic foot, then divide by 2,000.

Tons = cubic feet × density ÷ 2,000

Using 105 lb per cubic foot and the 176 cubic-foot allowance:

176 × 105 ÷ 2,000 = 9.24 tons

Density varies by gravel type, moisture, gradation, and supplier. Use the density supplied for the material you plan to purchase when available.

Multiple driveway layers

Many driveways use more than one aggregate layer. Calculate each layer using its own planned depth and material density, then add the results. Do not assume every layer has the same density or depth.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing inches and feet without converting.
  • Using the same density for different materials without checking supplier information.
  • Ignoring irregular edges or separate driveway sections.
  • Calculating total depth correctly but applying it to the wrong layer.
  • Ordering exactly the calculated amount without considering compaction, spreading, waste, or site variation.

Quick example

A 40 ft × 12 ft driveway with 4 inches of gravel is about 160 cubic feet before an allowance. With 10%, it is 176 cubic feet. At 105 lb per cubic foot, that is about 9.24 tons.

FAQ

How deep should driveway gravel be?

Depth depends on the driveway design, existing base, traffic, drainage, and aggregate type. Use the depth specified for your project rather than a generic assumption.

How do I convert cubic feet of gravel to tons?

Multiply cubic feet by the material density in pounds per cubic foot and divide by 2,000.

Why does gravel density matter?

Two gravel materials with the same volume can have different weights. Supplier density gives a better estimate of the amount sold by weight.

Should I calculate each driveway layer separately?

Yes. Separate layers can have different depths and material densities, so calculating them separately gives a clearer estimate.

Should I add extra gravel?

A planning allowance can account for variation, compaction, spreading, and waste. Confirm the final order quantity with your supplier or qualified professional.
