Mulch Bags vs Cubic Yards: How to Convert

Mulch can be sold in bags or by the cubic yard. The safest way to compare the two is to first calculate the volume your project needs, then use the bag's stated coverage or volume to estimate the number of bags.

Start with the project volume

Calculate the bed area and planned mulch depth first. For a rectangular bed:

Volume = length × width × depth in feet

If depth is in inches, divide it by 12 before calculating. For example, a 20 ft × 8 ft bed at 3 inches has a base volume of 40 cubic feet.

Convert cubic feet to cubic yards

One cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet:

Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27

So 40 cubic feet is about 1.48 cubic yards before any planning allowance.

Estimate the number of bags

Bag sizes and stated coverage vary. Use the volume or coverage printed on the bag rather than assuming every bag is the same.

If a bag states its volume in cubic feet:

Number of bags = required cubic feet ÷ bag volume

Round up to a whole bag because you cannot purchase a fraction of a bag.

If the manufacturer gives coverage in square feet at a specified depth, use that coverage directly:

Number of bags = project area ÷ stated coverage per bag

The stated coverage is tied to a particular depth, so do not use it at a different depth without adjusting the calculation.

Worked example

Suppose a 20 ft × 8 ft bed needs 3 inches of mulch. The base volume is 40 cubic feet, or about 1.48 cubic yards. With a 10% planning allowance, the estimate becomes 44 cubic feet, or about 1.63 cubic yards.

If a particular bag contains 2 cubic feet, 44 ÷ 2 = 22 bags. If another product has a different bag volume or coverage rating, its bag count will be different even though the project volume is the same.

Why bag coverage can be confusing

Coverage claims depend on the depth used for the calculation. A bag may cover a larger area at a shallow depth and a smaller area at a deeper depth. Always compare products using the same planned depth.

Common ordering mistakes

  • Assuming every mulch bag contains the same volume.
  • Using a coverage number without checking its stated depth.
  • Comparing bag counts without comparing bag volume or price.
  • Mixing cubic feet and cubic yards.
  • Forgetting that an irregular bed may need to be divided into sections.

FAQ

How many bags are in a cubic yard of mulch?

It depends on the volume of each bag. Divide 27 cubic feet by the bag's cubic-foot volume, then round up when purchasing whole bags.

Can I use bag coverage instead of bag volume?

Yes. If the manufacturer states coverage for a specific depth, divide your project area by that coverage and round up.

Why does mulch bag coverage change with depth?

A deeper layer uses more volume per square foot, so the same bag covers less area.

Should I add extra mulch?

A planning allowance may help with settling, measurement variation, and waste. The appropriate amount depends on the project.

Can I compare bagged and bulk mulch directly?

Yes, but compare equivalent volume and account for the supplier's stated bag size, bulk volume, and any delivery or handling differences.
