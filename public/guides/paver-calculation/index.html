How to Calculate Pavers for a Patio

Estimate how many pavers you need by calculating the project area, dividing it by the area covered by one paver, and adding a practical waste allowance.

Basic paver formula

For a rectangular project, first calculate the project area:

Project area = length × width

Then calculate the face area of one paver. Keep the units consistent; for example, convert both paver dimensions from inches to feet before calculating square feet:

Paver area = paver length × paver width

The basic quantity is:

Pavers needed = project area ÷ paver area

Finally, add your waste allowance:

Pavers to order = pavers needed × (1 + waste ÷ 100)

Round the final order quantity up to a whole paver.

Example: 12 ft × 20 ft patio with 6 in × 6 in pavers

A 12 ft by 20 ft patio has 240 sq ft of area. A 6 in by 6 in paver covers 0.25 sq ft because 6 inches equals 0.5 feet, and 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25.

So the base quantity is 240 ÷ 0.25 = 960 pavers.

With a 10% waste allowance:

960 × 1.10 = 1,056 pavers

For this example, plan to order 1,056 pavers.

Why add waste?

Extra material can cover cutting, breakage, damaged pieces, layout adjustments, and small measurement differences. The right allowance depends on the project shape, installation pattern, number of cuts, and material. A simple rectangular layout may need less waste than a curved or highly cut-up area.

What about joints?

This basic calculation uses the paver's face dimensions and does not separately model joint width. Actual coverage can vary with joint spacing and the manufacturer's stated coverage. For purchasing, compare your calculated quantity with the manufacturer's coverage per package or pallet.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing inches, feet, meters, or centimeters in the same calculation.
  • Forgetting to convert paver dimensions before calculating square feet.
  • Using the package quantity without checking the manufacturer's stated coverage.
  • Ignoring waste when the layout requires many cuts.
  • Rounding the base quantity down instead of rounding the final order quantity up.

FAQ

How many 6 × 6 inch pavers do I need for 100 square feet?

Each 6 × 6 inch paver covers 0.25 sq ft, so 100 sq ft requires 400 pavers before waste. Add your chosen waste allowance and round the final order quantity up.

How much waste should I add?

There is no single allowance for every project. Consider the layout, cutting, breakage, and material. The calculator lets you enter your own percentage rather than assuming one universal rate.

Does the calculator account for paver joints?

The basic calculator uses the paver face dimensions. Joint width can affect actual coverage, so check the manufacturer's coverage information for the specific paver.

Should I calculate by square feet or by pallets?

Calculate the required paver count first, then compare it with the manufacturer's package or pallet quantity and round up to complete packages when necessary.

Can I use this for a walkway or driveway?

Yes, the area calculation works for rectangular projects such as patios and walkways. Driveways and other load-bearing applications also require an appropriate base, paver specification, and installation method.
