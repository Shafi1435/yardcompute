How Much Concrete to Set a Fence Post?

The amount of concrete needed for one fence post depends mainly on the hole diameter and depth. There is no single bag count that fits every fence: post size, soil, frost conditions, wind exposure, local requirements, and the concrete product all affect the final quantity.

Quick answer: how much concrete per post?

For a planning example, a 12-inch diameter hole that is 24 inches deep holds about 1.57 cubic feet of material. With a 10% planning allowance, that becomes about 1.73 cubic feet per hole. The number of bags depends on the yield printed on the specific product bag.

For example, if a product states a yield of 0.60 cubic feet per bag, 1.73 ÷ 0.60 = 2.88, so you would round up to 3 bags per hole. Always use the manufacturer’s published yield rather than assuming every bag size has the same yield.

Estimating concrete for fence posts starts with the number of holes, the hole diameter, and the hole depth. Because a post hole is approximately cylindrical, its volume can be calculated from the circle area and depth.

1. Count the post holes

Start with the number of posts that will be set in concrete. Gates, corners, ends, and changes in direction can affect the actual post count, so use the number of holes you plan to dig rather than estimating from fence length alone.

2. Measure hole diameter and depth

Measure the planned hole diameter and depth. Keep the measurements in compatible units before calculating volume. If diameter and depth are in inches, convert the final cubic-inch result to cubic feet by dividing by 1,728.

3. Calculate one cylindrical hole

The volume of a cylinder is:

Volume = π × radius² × depth

The radius is half the hole diameter.

For a 12-inch diameter hole that is 24 inches deep:

π × 6² × 24 = about 2,714 cubic inches

That is about 1.57 cubic feet for one hole.

4. Multiply by the number of holes

For 10 identical holes:

1.57 × 10 = about 15.71 cubic feet

5. Add a planning allowance

A planning allowance can account for variation in hole size, spillage, uneven excavation, and other site conditions. With a 10% allowance:

15.71 × 1.10 = about 17.28 cubic feet

6. Estimate concrete bags

Bag yields vary by product, so use the yield printed on the bag or supplied by the manufacturer. Divide the required concrete volume by the yield per bag and round up to a whole bag.

Do not assume every 80-pound bag produces the same volume. Product formulations and published yields can differ.

Common mistakes

  • Using hole diameter as the radius.
  • Mixing inches and feet during the calculation.
  • Forgetting to multiply one-hole volume by the number of holes.
  • Using a bag count without checking the manufacturer's published yield.
  • Ignoring larger or unusual holes at gates, corners, or special posts.

FAQ

What size hole should I use?

Hole size depends on the post, fence design, soil, frost conditions, wind exposure, and local requirements. Use the dimensions specified for your project rather than a generic assumption.

How do I calculate the volume of one post hole?

Use π × radius² × depth, treating the hole as a cylinder.

How do I calculate concrete for several posts?

Calculate the volume of one hole and multiply it by the number of holes, then consider a planning allowance.

How many bags of concrete do I need?

Divide the required volume by the product's published yield per bag and round up.

Do all fence posts need identical holes?

Not necessarily. Gates, corners, end posts, and special structural conditions may use different dimensions. Calculate those holes separately when needed.
