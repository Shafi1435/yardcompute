How do I estimate a permeable paver project?

Permeable pavers have wider joints filled with small stone, so rainwater soaks through the surface into a deep base of washed, open-graded stone below. This calculator estimates the number of pavers from the area and paver size, and the volume of base aggregate from the area and base depth.

How to use the permeable paver calculator

  1. Enter the length and width of the area in feet.
  2. Enter the paver length and width in inches.
  3. Enter the total base depth in inches from your design or the manufacturer's guide.
  4. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Project area = length × width
Paver area (sq ft) = (paver length ÷ 12) × (paver width ÷ 12)
Pavers to order = round up (area ÷ paver area × (1 + waste % ÷ 100))
Base aggregate (cu ft) = area × (base depth ÷ 12) × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)

Example

The default values describe a 20 ft × 12 ft area using 6 × 6 in pavers on an 8 in base. The area is 240 sq ft, and each paver covers 0.25 sq ft, so the area holds 960 pavers, or 1,056 with 10% extra. The base needs 240 × 8/12 = 160 cu ft, or 176 cu ft (about 6.52 cubic yards) with the allowance.

How permeable systems differ

  • Layered stone base. Permeable systems often use more than one layer of washed, open-graded stone, such as a coarser lower layer and a finer upper layer. Calculate each layer's depth separately if your design has several.
  • Bedding and joint stone. Instead of sand, the pavers usually sit on a thin layer of small washed stone, and the same kind of stone fills the joints. This calculator does not estimate those layers.
  • Deeper base. Because the base stores water, it is often deeper than a standard paver base. The right depth depends on soil, rainfall, and traffic.
  • Joint spacers. The wider joints are built into the paver shape, so the paver count is based on the paver's laying dimensions. Use the dimensions the manufacturer lists for coverage.

Check the design

Permeable paving is part of the drainage system. Soil that drains poorly, steep slopes, or areas with vehicle traffic may need an engineered design, an underdrain, or a different base. Some areas also have stormwater rules or incentives. Follow the manufacturer's design guide and check local requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the base so deep?

The open-graded stone holds rainwater temporarily while it soaks into the soil below. More storage usually means a deeper base.

Can I use regular paver base gravel?

Usually not. Standard base gravel contains fines that pack tightly and slow drainage. Permeable systems use washed stone without fines.

How many tons of base stone is this?

Use the base volume with the supplier's density in the Gravel Tonnage Calculator.

Can I use metric?

Yes. Switch the area to meters and paver size and base depth to centimeters.

Do I need edge restraint?

Yes, most permeable paver systems need a sturdy edge. Use the Paver Edge Restraint Calculator.
