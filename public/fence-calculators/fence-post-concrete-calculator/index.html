How much concrete do I need to set a fence post?

Your estimate depends on the hole diameter, hole depth, number of posts, and the actual yield of the concrete mix. A cylindrical hole 12 inches in diameter and 24 inches deep holds about 1.57 cubic feet before accounting for the post itself.

Enter the number of holes, their diameter, and their depth. This calculator reports the full cylindrical hole volume and approximate bag counts using planning yields of 0.60 cubic feet per 80-lb bag and 0.45 cubic feet per 60-lb bag. It does not automatically add a waste allowance.

Formula for fence-post concrete

Radius (ft) = hole diameter (in) ÷ 24
Depth (ft) = hole depth (in) ÷ 12
One hole (cu ft) = π × radius² × depth
Total hole volume = one-hole volume × number of posts
Estimated bags = ceiling(total volume ÷ yield per bag)

The bag count rounds up to a whole bag. The 0.60 and 0.45 cu ft yields are estimates for comparison, not guarantees for every brand or mix. Use the yield stated on the product label or by the supplier when available.

Worked example: 10 fence posts

Suppose you are setting 10 posts in holes that are each 12 inches in diameter and 24 inches deep.

  • Radius: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 inches, or 0.5 ft.
  • Volume per hole: π × 0.5² × 2 = about 1.57 cu ft.
  • Total hole volume: 1.57 × 10 = about 15.71 cu ft before post displacement.
  • 80-lb bags: 15.71 ÷ 0.60 = 26.18, rounded up to about 27 bags.
  • 60-lb bags: 15.71 ÷ 0.45 = 34.91, rounded up to about 35 bags.

These figures are the calculator's baseline estimate, not a guarantee of the quantity to buy. Add a separate allowance if your holes are irregular, the soil crumbles, or concrete is likely to spill; confirm the final count using the specific product yield.

Assumptions and material notes

The calculator treats every hole as a perfect cylinder and does not subtract the volume occupied by a post, so the volume can be conservative. Real holes may be wider at the top, uneven, or enlarged during digging. Actual concrete needs can also change with post dimensions and the installation method.

Concrete bag weights do not directly determine yield: different mixes and products can produce different volumes. Compare the published yield in cubic feet per bag rather than assuming every 60-lb or 80-lb bag is identical.

Frequently asked questions

How much concrete do I need for one fence post?

Measure the hole diameter and depth first. A 12-inch-diameter by 24-inch-deep cylindrical hole is about 1.57 cubic feet before a planning allowance or post displacement.

Does the estimate account for the fence post itself?

No. It uses the full cylindrical hole volume, so it can be conservative.

Why can bag counts vary by brand?

Concrete products have different published yields and mix characteristics.

Should I add extra concrete?

For purchasing, follow the product yield and allow for excavation variation and spillage.

Can I use decimal hole sizes?

Yes.

What hole size should I use?

Use the hole dimensions required by your post size, soil conditions, frost depth, and local installation guidance.
