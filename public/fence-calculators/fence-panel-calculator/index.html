How to estimate fence panels and posts

Measure the total straight fence line in feet, then check the actual width of the panels you plan to buy. Panel widths are not universal: common products are sold in nominal widths such as 6 or 8 feet, but the manufacturer’s stated installed width and the space required for posts determine the layout. Enter the measured fence length and the panel width into the calculator.

Fence panel calculator formula

Panels needed = round up (fence length ÷ panel width). The calculator rounds up because a partial final span still needs a panel or a cut panel. For one continuous straight run with a post at each end and one post between adjacent panels, line posts = panels needed + 1. The optional waste allowance is applied to the panel order quantity, not the post count.

Worked example: 100-foot fence with 8-foot panels

Divide 100 feet by 8 feet per panel to get 12.5 spans. Rounding up gives 13 panels. A straight run with 13 panel spans generally needs 14 line posts when each panel is supported between two posts. With a 10% extra-panel allowance, 13 × 1.10 = 14.3, which rounds up to 15 panels to order. Actual fit depends on the product’s installed width and the final cut.

Planning assumptions and limitations

  • This estimate assumes a single straight run and standard panel-to-post layout.
  • Gates, corners, changes in direction, uneven ground, and stepped or racked panels can change post and panel quantities.
  • Some systems use shared posts, brackets, or special end and corner posts; follow the manufacturer’s layout instructions.
  • Waste allowance adds panels for cuts or damage. It does not automatically account for spare posts, hardware, concrete, or gate kits.

Fence panel calculator FAQs

How many 8-foot fence panels do I need for 100 feet?

Divide 100 by 8 and round up: 13 panels cover the nominal length before checking installed widths and the final cut. A 10% extra-panel allowance raises the order estimate to 15 panels.

How many posts do I need for fence panels?

For one straight run with a post at both ends and one post between each adjacent panel, estimate one more line post than the number of panel spans. A 13-panel run therefore typically uses 14 line posts. Gates and corners require a separate layout.

Should I include extra panels?

An allowance can help cover cuts, accidental damage, or a future repair. Choose the percentage based on the fence layout and product rather than treating it as a fixed rule.

Do all fence panels have the same width?

No. Measure the actual installed width in the product specifications. Nominal width, bracket spacing, post dimensions, and required gaps can affect the number that fit.

Does this calculator include gates and corner posts?

No. It estimates a straight panel run and basic line-post count. Measure gate openings and every corner or direction change separately, then follow the fence system’s installation instructions.

Can I use this estimate on sloped ground?

Use it as a first estimate only. Sloped ground may require stepped panels, racked panels, different post lengths, or extra cuts. Confirm the intended layout before ordering.
