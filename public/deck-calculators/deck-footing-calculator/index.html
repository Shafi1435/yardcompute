How much concrete do deck footings need?

Most freestanding and attached decks rest on concrete piers or footings under each post. Each footing is roughly a cylinder in the ground. This calculator finds the concrete in one footing from its diameter and depth, multiplies by the number of footings, adds waste, and estimates 80 lb bags.

How to use the deck footing calculator

  1. Count the footings on your deck plan. Each post usually has one.
  2. Enter the footing diameter in inches.
  3. Enter the depth of concrete in inches.
  4. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Volume of one footing = π × (diameter ÷ 24)² × (depth ÷ 12)
Base concrete = one footing × number of footings
Order volume = base × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)
Approx. 80 lb bags = round up (order volume ÷ 0.60)

Example

The default values describe 6 footings, each 12 in across and 36 in deep. One footing holds π × 0.5² × 3 = 2.36 cu ft, so six hold 14.14 cu ft. With 10% extra, that is 15.55 cu ft, or about 0.58 cubic yards, which is about 26 bags at 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag.

A larger deck with 8 of the same footings needs 18.85 cu ft before waste and about 35 bags with 10% extra.

How many footings does a deck need?

The number depends on beam size, beam span, post spacing, and whether the deck is attached to the house with a ledger. A small attached deck might need only one beam line with a few posts, while a large freestanding deck needs more. Your deck plan or local building department will set the post layout; this calculator only turns that layout into concrete.

Deck footing tips

  • Footing size and depth are set by soil type, deck load, and frost depth. Requirements vary by location, so confirm them before you dig.
  • Many decks use a wider footing pad at the bottom of the hole. If yours does, add that volume separately.
  • Post bases or anchors are set into the top of the footing. Have them ready before the pour.
  • Holes dug with an auger often come out a little wider than the bit. Measure and use the real diameter.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as the Concrete Footing Calculator?

The formula is the same. This page has deck-specific defaults and notes. The Concrete Footing Calculator is set up for general round footings.

Can I use precast deck blocks instead?

Some low, freestanding decks use precast blocks where allowed. Whether they are acceptable depends on local code and the deck design.

Should the footing stick out above the ground?

Often the top is set a little above grade so water drains away from the post base. Follow your plan and the post-base manufacturer's instructions.

Can I enter metric sizes?

Yes. Switch diameter and depth to centimeters.
