How many deck screws do I need?

When deck boards are face-screwed, each board is fastened where it crosses a joist, usually with two screws. This calculator counts the deck boards across the deck width, counts how many joists each board crosses, multiplies by the screws per crossing, and adds a waste allowance for dropped, stripped, or broken screws.

How to use the deck screw calculator

  1. Enter the deck length as the direction the boards run, and the deck width as the direction they are laid across.
  2. Enter the actual board width and the gap between boards.
  3. Enter the joist spacing on center.
  4. Enter the screws used at each joist crossing. Two is typical for face screwing.
  5. Set a waste allowance and select Calculate.

Formula

Boards = round up ((deck width × 12 + gap) ÷ (board width + gap))
Joist crossings per board = round up ((deck length × 12) ÷ joist spacing) + 1
Screws to order = round up (boards × crossings × screws per crossing × (1 + waste % ÷ 100))

Example

The default values describe a 16 ft × 12 ft deck with 5.5 in boards, 1/8 in gaps, joists at 16 in on center, and 2 screws per crossing. The 12 ft width needs 26 boards: (144 + 0.125) ÷ 5.625 = 25.6, rounded up. Each 16 ft board crosses 192 ÷ 16 + 1 = 13 joists. That is 26 × 13 × 2 = 676 screws, or 744 screws with 10% extra.

Turning screws into boxes

Deck screws are sold by count or by weight, and the number per box depends on screw length and gauge. Divide the result by the count printed on the box and round up. If the box is labeled only by weight, check the packaging or the manufacturer's listing for the approximate screw count.

When this estimate changes

  • Hidden fastener systems use clips between boards instead of face screws. Follow the clip manufacturer's coverage chart instead.
  • Butt joints and picture framing add extra screws where board ends meet or where border boards are fastened.
  • Closer joist spacing means more crossings per board, so more screws.
  • Framing screws and structural connectors for joists, hangers, and ledgers are separate and must be rated for that use.

Frequently asked questions

Why two screws per joist?

Two screws per crossing help hold each board flat and reduce cupping. Some wide boards or specific products call for a different number, so check the decking instructions.

What kind of screws should I use?

Use screws made for exterior decking and compatible with your board material. Treated lumber, composite, and hardwood can each need different coatings or screw types. Follow the decking manufacturer's recommendation.

Does this include screws for stairs and railings?

No. Stairs, railings, and fascia boards need their own fastener counts.

How much extra should I buy?

A 5–10% allowance is a common planning range. Buying a little extra is usually cheaper than a second trip to the store mid-project.

Can I use metric inputs?

Yes. Switch the deck dimensions to meters and the board width, gap, and joist spacing to centimeters, and the calculator converts them.
