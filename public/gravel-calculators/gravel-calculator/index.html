How the gravel calculator works

Enter the area dimensions, depth, and an estimated gravel density. The calculator estimates base volume, applies a 10% planning allowance, and converts the order volume to an approximate weight using the density you enter.

Formula

Base volume = length × width × depth in feet
Order volume = base volume × 1.10
Tons = order volume × density ÷ 2,000

Example

A 20 ft × 10 ft area at 3 inches deep is about 50 cubic feet before the planning allowance. At 105 lb/cu ft, the estimated order weight is about 2.89 tons.

Assumptions and material notes

Gravel density varies by rock type, grading, moisture, and supplier. Use the supplier's published density or weight-per-cubic-yard information when available.

How to use the gravel calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the area in feet.
  2. Enter the depth of gravel in inches.
  3. Enter your supplier's density in lb per cu ft, or keep 105 as a planning value.
  4. Select Calculate to see the volume, volume with 10% extra, and approximate tons.

Worked example in full

The default 20 ft × 10 ft area at 3 in deep holds 50 cu ft of gravel. The 10% allowance raises that to 55 cu ft, which is about 2.04 cubic yards. At 105 lb per cu ft, 55 × 105 = 5,775 lb, or about 2.89 tons.

A 4 ft × 40 ft path at 3 in deep is 40 cu ft, or 44 cu ft with the allowance, which is about 2.31 tons at the same density.

Common gravel depths

  • Decorative beds over fabric: often 2–3 in.
  • Walkways and patios: a compacted base plus a top layer; total depth depends on soil and use.
  • Driveways: much deeper, usually in layers. Use the Driveway Gravel Calculator.
  • French drains: use the French Drain Gravel Calculator, which works from trench size.

Buying by the yard, ton, or bag

Bulk suppliers sell by the cubic yard or the ton; stores sell bags. This calculator shows volume and weight so you can compare. For bag counts, use the Gravel Bag Calculator; for tons without the built-in allowance, use the Gravel Tonnage Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Why does density matter?

The same volume of different aggregates can have different weights.

Is 105 lb/cu ft universal?

No. It is only a default planning value. Replace it with your supplier's value when available.

Why include 10%?

It provides a simple allowance for minor variation and settlement; actual needs vary by project.

Can I enter decimals?

Yes.

Should I verify the gravel density?

Yes. Enter the density from your supplier or material specification when available because different aggregates have different weights.

Can I enter metric sizes?

Yes. Switch length and width to meters, depth to centimeters, and density to kg/m³.
