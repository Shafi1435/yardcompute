How many tons of gravel do I need?

Landscape and quarry suppliers often sell gravel by the ton, but you measure your project in feet. This calculator finds the gravel volume for a rectangular area and converts it to tons using the material's density, the weight of one cubic foot.

How to use the gravel tonnage calculator

  1. Enter the length and width of the area in feet.
  2. Enter the gravel depth in inches.
  3. Enter the density of your gravel in pounds per cubic foot. Ask your supplier for the figure for the exact material.
  4. Select Calculate.

Formula

Volume (cu ft) = length × width × (depth ÷ 12)
Cubic yards = volume ÷ 27
Tons = volume × density ÷ 2,000

Example

The default values describe a 20 ft × 10 ft area, 3 in deep, with gravel at 105 lb per cu ft. The volume is 50 cu ft, or 1.85 cubic yards. 50 × 105 = 5,250 lb, which is about 2.63 tons.

At 4 in deep, the same area needs 66.67 cu ft, or 2.47 cubic yards, which weighs about 3.50 tons.

Why density matters

Different stone weighs different amounts. Crushed stone with fines packs tightly and weighs more per cubic foot; washed, rounded stone has more air gaps. Moisture adds weight too. A difference of 10 lb per cu ft changes the tonnage by about 10%, so a supplier's own density or tons-per-yard figure gives the best estimate.

Add your own allowance

This calculator gives the tonnage for the exact volume and does not add waste. Gravel compacts and spreads unevenly, so many people round up or add 5–10% before ordering. Suppliers may also sell only in set increments such as half or whole tons.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

It depends on the material, but many gravels fall in a range of roughly 1.3 to 1.5 tons per cubic yard. At 105 lb per cu ft, one cubic yard weighs about 1.42 tons.

Should I order by the ton or the yard?

Order in whatever unit your supplier uses. This calculator shows both so you can compare quotes.

Does compaction change the amount?

Yes. Compacted base gravel takes more loose material than the finished depth suggests. Ask your supplier about compaction for base layers.

Can I enter metric density?

Yes. Switch density to kg/m³ and dimensions to meters and centimeters; the result still shows US tons.

Can a pickup truck carry this?

Payload limits vary widely by vehicle. Check your vehicle's rated payload before hauling gravel yourself.
