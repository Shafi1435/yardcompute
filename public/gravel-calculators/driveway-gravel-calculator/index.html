How much gravel do I need for a driveway?

Enter the driveway length, width, depth, and estimated gravel density. The calculator converts the dimensions to volume, adds a 10% planning allowance, reports cubic yards, and estimates weight in tons. If your driveway uses different aggregate layers, calculate each layer separately.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the driveway length and average width.
  2. Enter the planned depth of the gravel layer.
  3. Use your supplier's published density when available.
  4. Review cubic feet, cubic yards, and estimated tons.
  5. For a multi-layer driveway, repeat the calculation for each material and depth.

Formula

Base volume = length × width × depth in feet
Order volume = base volume × (1 + waste ÷ 100)
Tons = order volume × density ÷ 2,000

Example

A 40 ft × 12 ft driveway at 4 inches deep has a base volume of 160 cubic feet, or about 5.93 cubic yards. At an estimated density of 105 lb/cu ft, a 10% planning allowance gives about 9.24 tons. Actual supplier weight can differ because aggregate density and moisture vary.

Driveway depth and layers

A gravel driveway may use more than one aggregate layer, with different materials or thicknesses for the sub-base, base, and surface. There is no single depth that fits every site. Soil conditions, drainage, traffic, climate, and local construction practice all matter. Use project-specific specifications rather than treating a generic depth as a universal rule.

Why supplier density matters

Two gravel products with the same volume can weigh different amounts. Density can also vary with grading and moisture. When buying by the ton, ask the supplier for the material's current approximate density or coverage information and compare it with your calculated volume.

Common planning mistakes

  • Using one depth for a driveway that actually has multiple layers.
  • Using a generic density when the supplier provides a better estimate.
  • Measuring only one width on a driveway that changes width.
  • Ignoring grading, low spots, drainage, or an uneven subgrade.
  • Ordering the mathematical minimum with no allowance for site conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include multiple driveway layers?

No. Calculate each layer separately when different materials or depths are used.

What density should I use?

Use your supplier's published density when available. The density input is used to convert volume to an estimated weight.

Is 10% waste always enough?

No. The allowance is a planning input, not a guarantee. Uneven subgrades, grading, delivery conditions, and material loss can require a different allowance.

Can I use decimal depths?

Yes. Decimal measurements are supported, and you can choose inches, feet, or metric units where available.

Should I calculate each driveway layer separately?

Yes. Give each layer its own thickness, material density, and planning allowance.

Can this estimate truckloads?

The current calculator reports volume and estimated tons. Truckload count depends on the supplier's truck capacity and should be calculated from the supplier's stated payload.

Why does my supplier's tonnage differ?

Aggregate density varies by material, grading, moisture, and compaction. Supplier estimates may also include their own coverage assumptions.

Should I verify the quantity before ordering?

Yes. Confirm measurements, material type, density, delivery minimums, and site conditions with your supplier or contractor.
