How much pea gravel do I need?

Measure the length and width of the area and choose a finished gravel depth. This calculator estimates the base volume, adds your selected waste allowance, converts the order quantity to cubic yards and approximate tons, and estimates material cost using the price per ton you enter.

Pea gravel calculator formula

Area (square feet) = length × width.

Base volume (cubic feet) = area × depth in inches ÷ 12.

Order volume = base volume × (1 + waste percentage ÷ 100).

Cubic yards = order volume ÷ 27.

Estimated tons = order volume × density in pounds per cubic foot ÷ 2,000.

Estimated material cost = estimated tons × your supplier price per ton.

Worked example: 20 ft by 10 ft area

For a 20 ft × 10 ft area at 2 inches deep, the project covers 200 square feet. The base volume is 33.33 cubic feet. With a 10% planning allowance, the order volume is about 36.67 cubic feet, or 1.36 cubic yards. At an illustrative density of 105 lb/cu ft, that is about 1.93 tons. If your supplier quotes $50 per ton, estimated material cost is about $96.25 before delivery, taxes, or other fees.

How to estimate pea gravel cost

Ask your supplier whether the quote is priced by the ton, cubic yard, or bag. If the quote is per ton, enter that price in the calculator. The example price is only an editable illustration, not a current market quote. If you receive a cubic-yard or per-bag price, convert the quote to a comparable unit before estimating your material total.

Choosing pea gravel depth

Depth depends on the project. A decorative path, play area, drainage layer, and planting bed may require different specifications. Use the depth recommended for your intended use and check whether the supplier recommends a compacted or loose depth. Do not assume one depth is suitable for every installation.

Density, waste, and delivery assumptions

Pea gravel weight varies with source, grading, moisture, and supplier specifications. The default density is a planning input; replace it with supplier data when available. The waste allowance is also adjustable and should reflect your layout, edges, uneven ground, and installation method.

The material-cost result excludes delivery, minimum-load charges, taxes, site preparation, edging, fabric, labor, and equipment. Suppliers may sell by different units and may round orders to full loads or bags, so confirm the final quantity and price before purchasing.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pea gravel cost per ton?

Prices vary by location, supplier, order size, delivery distance, gravel source, and season. Ask local suppliers for a delivered or pickup quote, then enter the comparable per-ton material price in the calculator.

How many cubic yards are in a ton of pea gravel?

It depends on the material density. To estimate cubic yards from tons, convert tons to pounds, divide by density in pounds per cubic foot, then divide cubic feet by 27. Use the density supplied by your gravel provider.

How deep should pea gravel be?

There is no single depth for every project. Choose a depth based on the intended use and installation guidance, and verify it with your supplier or installer.

Does this calculator include delivery?

No. It estimates material cost from the entered price per ton. Delivery, taxes, minimum-load fees, labor, and site preparation are not included.

Can I use a price per cubic yard or bag?

This calculator's cost field is price per ton. If your supplier quotes another unit, convert the quote to a per-ton equivalent using the supplier's density or packaging information before entering it.

Why does pea gravel density matter?

Density converts volume into approximate weight. Moisture, grading, and source can change the actual weight, so use supplier specifications when possible.
